The increasing focus on non-dollar stablecoins signals a potential diversification of digital commercial money, offering new avenues for cross-border liquidity and potentially reducing reliance on USD-pegged instruments for specific corridors. This will impact commercial bank balance sheet strategies and foreign exchange risk management.

## 3. STRUCTURAL & OPERATIONAL PAIN POINTS
*   **Interoperability Silos:** The [IMF](https://www.coindesk.com/business/2026/10/11/imf-finds-demand-for-tokenized-stocks-says-the-market-is-still-volatile-illiquid) notes that while there is demand for tokenised stocks, "liquidity, legal rules and settlement systems have not caught up," indicating fragmentation across nascent tokenised markets. This prevents seamless, atomic settlement and creates isolated pools of liquidity across varied DLTs and asset classes, hindering the development of a unified wholesale market.
*   **Balance Sheet & Liquidity Friction:** The [IMF's](https://www.coindesk.com/business/2026/10/11/imf-finds-demand-for-tokenized-stocks-says-the-market-is-still-volatile,illiquid) observation of 'volatile, illiquid' tokenised stock markets highlights a significant hurdle for commercial banks and treasuries, as unpredictable liquidity undermines efficient capital allocation and necessitates higher liquidity buffers. The slow development of legal and settlement frameworks for tokenised assets can constrain balance sheets by creating regulatory uncertainty and limiting their eligibility for established collateral management processes, impacting 24/7 intraday liquidity management.
*   **Post-Trade Plumbing Constraints:** The [IMF](https://www.coindesk.com/business/2026/10/11/imf-finds-demand-for-tokenized-stocks-says-the-market-is-still-volatile-illiquid) explicitly states that 'settlement systems have not caught up' with the demand for tokenised stocks, pointing to the underlying legacy post-trade infrastructure being ill-equipped for the 24/7, atomic settlement capabilities promised by DLT. This creates bottlenecks for Delivery vs Payment (DvP) and Payment vs Payment (PvP), prolongs settlement cycles, and adds friction for custodians and other market infrastructure providers seeking to integrate digital assets into existing workflows, increasing operational risk and cost.
